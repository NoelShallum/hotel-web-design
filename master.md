# Master Doc: Himalayan Wellness Retreat

Source: founder's voice-note transcriptions (parts 1 to 3). This doc condenses everything said so far. Anything not stated by the founder is listed under **Open questions**, not assumed.

## 1. Name

**Shivaya Prakriti: An Ananda Kedar Experience**

- Brand name: Shivaya Prakriti
- Tagline or descriptor: An Ananda Kedar Experience

## 2. The concept

A wellness retreat, stay, hotel and café in the Himalayas. It combines:

- **Wellness retreat programs** (healing, yoga, sound therapy)
- **Boutique stay**: a homestay-style property that also works as a workstation for long stays
- **Café** serving fresh, hygienic food
- **Community and craft**: art and making workshops, and local village development

**Core idea:** "going back to the roots."

## 3. Location

- **Status:** not finalised. The founder is still evaluating options.
- **Most likely:** Tirthan Valley, Himachal Pradesh.
- **Access:** the valley branches off at the Aut tunnel, about 480 km from Delhi NCR.
- The property itself is also not finalised.

## 4. Property

- **Rooms:** 5 to 6 minimum.
- **Setting:** forests, river and fresh water, with good air quality (AQI reported as good).
- **Use:** short retreats and long stays. Guests can work remotely from the property.

## 5. Programs and offerings

### Wellness retreat program
- **Sound healing therapy**, run by the founder.
- **Naturopathic healing**, with a professional practitioner.
- **Vedic yoga**, with a practitioner. The founder will also host yoga sessions.
- **Freelance practitioners** onboarded and hosted, building a healing community.

### Art and making workshops
- **Clay modelling** (pottery-style; the founder noted a trendy activity but did not recall the name).
- **Culinary plate design** (preparing and designing plates).
- **Painting and building something new.**
- **Building and construction** (property-related, hands-on).
- **Volunteering.**
- **Hemp** crafts and products.
- **Upcycling:** turning waste generated on site into useful things ("best out of waste").

### Long stays and workstation
- Guests stay for longer periods, work remotely, and breathe clean mountain air.

### Future idea: body impact devices
- At some point, devices that show guests the effect of clean air on their bodies. Examples mentioned were measuring carbon dioxide and pollutant exposure from city life versus after time in the mountains.
- **Timing:** later, not at launch.

## 6. Food and café

- Fresh food with strong **hygiene SOPs**.
- Grow most vegetables on site, or buy from local vendors.

## 7. Community and local impact

- Work with nearby villages on their growth and development.
- Give villages business by buying **milk, silk, fruit and vegetables** from them.
- Aim to help build up the area, as part of it.

## 8. Sustainability

- Sustainability is a core theme.
- Plastic used day to day must be disposed of properly or **recycled**.
- On-site waste should be upcycled where possible (ties into the workshops).
- Grow food locally and buy locally.

## 9. Brand and website themes

Themes to carry through the site, drawn from the founder's words:

- Back to the roots
- Healing and community
- Local and sustainable
- Clean air, trees, river
- Hands-on creativity

### Visual style direction

Reference images and links live in [references/](./references/). Current direction, from the first reference: warm, communal and moody, with forest greens, earth browns and amber firelight, set among tall trees. Details in [references/README.md](./references/README.md).

> **Direction chosen (2026-10-12): Fireside Dusk.** The founder set aside Indian Brutalism and picked Fireside Dusk from the options in [references/inspiration-research.md](./references/inspiration-research.md). The main audience is **Delhi weekenders and international guests**. The list headed "Earlier decisions" further down is the Brutalism record and is superseded.

**Current direction: Fireside Dusk**
- **Approach:** photography-led and cinematic. Night-forest sections for mood, cream paper sections for reading, ember used sparingly for the main action. The only ornament is a thin inset frame on photos (after Pahari miniature borders) and a small diamond rule. Devanagari is always paired with English.
- **Audience:** Delhi weekenders and international guests. English first, Devanagari as accent. The page answers "what is it like and how do I get there", and marks anything unknown with a `[TBC]` chip.
- **Colour:**

  | Role | Name | Hex |
  |---|---|---|
  | Dark ground | Night | `#0C130F` |
  | Dark panels | Pine | `#15241B` |
  | Light ground | Cream | `#F3E9D6` |
  | Light ground, alternate | Paper | `#EADDC3` |
  | Text on light | Ink | `#17231B` |
  | Secondary text on dark | Mist | `#C2BEAC` |
  | Main action, fills only | Ember | `#E8762B` |
  | Highlights and labels on dark | Glow | `#F4A24B` |
  | Labels and links on light | Coal | `#A63B22` |

  Rules: Ember is never text on cream (2.5:1). Button text is Night on Ember (6.3:1). Cream on Night is 15.6:1. Coal on Cream is 5.3:1.
- **Type:** Fraunces for display (weights 300 to 500), Noto Serif Devanagari for Devanagari, Hind for body, Kalam only for short handwritten notes. Devanagari is set at the same size as sentence-case Latin with more leading.
- **Photography:** free placeholder photos for the mock, credited in [site/assets/img/photos/CREDITS.md](./site/assets/img/photos/CREDITS.md). None shows the property. The hero is people around a fire at dusk in the Himalayan foothills. Replace all of it with real photography before launch.

**Earlier decisions (Indian Brutalism, superseded)**
- **Approach:** "Indian Brutalism": neubrutalist structure (heavy type, thick outlines, hard shadows) with rustic Himachali and Sanskrit cues (Kath-Kuni stone-and-timber banding, Devanagari headlines).
- **Type (confirmed): Signboard.** Eczar ExtraBold for display (Latin and Devanagari), Hind for body, Kalam for short handwritten notes.
- **Colour (final): "Sandhya" (संध्या, twilight).** Purples plus two oranges, with ivory and pure black. It follows the 60/30/10 rule:

  | Role | Name | Hex |
  |---|---|---|
  | 60% Trust | Dark Amethyst | `#240046` |
  | 60% Trust, secondary | Indigo Velvet | `#5A189A` |
  | 60% Trust, tertiary | Royal Violet | `#7B2CBF` |
  | 30% Structure | Ivory white | `#FFFFF0` |
  | 10% Action | Pumpkin Spice | `#FF6D00` |
  | 10% Action, highlight | Amber Glow | `#FF9E00` |
  | Text, outlines, shadows | Black | `#000000` |

  **Rules of use**
  - One Pumpkin button per screen.
  - Text on either orange is always black. Never ivory on orange, and never orange text on ivory (2.8:1).
  - Amber is for highlights on the dark purples.
  - Violet is for links and icons on ivory, never for text on Amethyst (2.5:1).
  - Every block gets a 3px black outline and a hard offset shadow.
  - The Kath-Kuni stripe alternates Indigo and Violet bands separated by black "mortar" lines.

  **Tokens:** `--amethyst:#240046; --indigo:#5A189A; --violet:#7B2CBF; --pumpkin:#FF6D00; --amber:#FF9E00; --ivory:#FFFFF0; --black:#000000;`

  **Caution:** both oranges sit close to saffron, which carries political associations in India today. Next to purple they read as sunset.
- Earlier palette rounds (Deodar, Neel, Shila, Mor Pankh, Jamun, Mehendi, Shila Vivid) were exploration only. The design canvas with all boards is linked from [references/README.md](./references/README.md).

### Brand guidelines

Full guidelines (v1.0) are in [references/brand-guidelines.md](./references/brand-guidelines.md). The short version:

- **Wordmark:** "Shivaya Prakriti" in Eczar 800, with the Devanagari line शिवाय प्रकृति and the descriptor in Kalam. A proposal until a logo is supplied.
- **Colour:** the Sandhya palette above, split 60/30/10. Every text and background pair on the site comes from the approved contrast table.
- **Type:** Eczar for headlines, Hind for body, Kalam for short notes. Devanagari is always paired with English.
- **Components:** 3px black outlines, hard offset shadows (no blur, no radius), one Pumpkin button per screen, Kath-Kuni stripe as the only texture. On dark purple surfaces the outline and shadow flip to ivory or Amber.
- **Imagery:** warm, communal, moody, with real people and natural materials. The campfire reference is a placeholder, not the property.
- **Voice:** plain, warm, specific. No medical claims. Unconfirmed facts show as an amber `[TBC]` chip instead of invented details.

## 11. Competitor analysis

**Plan:** benchmark against **Zostel** and **The Hosteller**.

**Model they use (tripartite agreement):** the brand takes a property on lease, or works with the owner or investor, and handles marketing, operations and social media. The brand or an investor may fund the build-out. The founder wants to understand this model as a possible route or comparison.

**Figures quoted by the founder (unverified, to be checked against sources):**

| Brand | Occupancy | Break-even |
|---|---|---|
| Zostel | 60 to 70% | About 18 months |
| The Hosteller | About 70% | Not stated |

**To look at:** how much a brand's arrival lifts occupancy in a local area, and what it offers guests.
