## 1. Brand at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Shivaya Prakriti |
| **Descriptor** | An Ananda Kedar Experience |
| **Devanagari** | शिवाय प्रकृति |
| **What it is** | A small wellness retreat, boutique stay and café in the Himalayas |
| **Core idea** | Going back to the roots |
| **Audience** | Delhi weekenders and international guests |
| **Look** | Fireside Dusk: photography first, night-forest dark with ember light, cream paper for reading, almost no ornament |
| **Feeling we want** | Warm, unhurried, grounded and hands-on. A place you arrive at dusk and are glad you came |

**Themes to carry through every page**
- Back to the roots
- Healing and community
- Local and sustainable
- Clean air, trees, river
- Hands-on creativity

**What the brand is not**
- A luxury-spa lookalike. Dark photo, thin gold serif and a booking bar describe Ananda, Atmantan, Six Senses and most of the category. We are a craft and community place, and should not read as a hotel chain.
- Loud. The earlier Brutalism direction was set aside as too heavy for a place about slowing down.
- Purple or neon. The Sandhya palette is retired.
- Generic "wellness". Every line names a real thing: clay, pine, milk, the river.

**Who we are writing for**
- **Delhi weekenders:** short on time, tired of the city, want a real break and clear logistics. They compare us with hill stays and hostels, and want to know how far it is and what the days look like.
- **International guests:** read English, may not know Indian wellness terms, need plain logistics (airport, road, visas, payment) before they trust a booking. Devanagari reads to them as place and character, so it is always glossed in English.

## 2. The idea behind the look

The page is one evening. It opens at dusk beside a fire, moves through the night forest, and comes back to warm light on cream paper when you need to read something. Dark sections carry mood and photography. Cream sections carry reading and decisions. Ember, the colour of a fire, is saved for the one thing we want guests to do: ask to stay.

Three decisions follow from the research:
1. **Let photography lead.** The sites that feel owned by an Indian source (Raw Mango, Anokhi, Nicobar) use real material and keep the interface quiet. We do the same.
2. **Take the ornament from Himachal, lightly.** Pahari miniatures sit inside thin ruled borders. Our photos get a thin inset rule, and lists get a small diamond. Nothing else is decorative.
3. **Show Devanagari with confidence, but never alone.** Almost no site in this category puts Devanagari in its interface. We do, always next to English.

Hairlines are Cream at 16% on dark and Ink at 20% on light. Photo frames use Cream at 50%.

### 4.2 Proportions

Aim for roughly 55 to 60% Night and Pine, 30 to 35% Cream and Paper, and under 10% Ember and Glow. Count over the whole page, not per section. Ember never fills a large area. Its largest uses are a pill button and a thin glow at the bottom of the hero.

### 4.3 Rules of use

1. **One Ember action per section.** Ember is "plan your stay", "enquire", "send enquiry". The header's small Enquire button repeats the page's main action. Secondary actions use the ghost button (on dark) or the Ink button (on light).
2. **Ember is a fill, not text.** Never Ember text on Cream (2.5:1) or Paper (2.2:1). On dark it passes (6.3:1), but use Glow for text.
3. **Text on Ember is Night** (6.3:1) or Ink (5.5:1). Never Cream on Ember (2.5:1).
4. **Labels, links, notes and error text on light grounds are Coal.** Never Glow or Ember (Glow on Cream is 1.7:1).
5. **Secondary text is Mist on dark and Ink 2 on light.** Never Ink 2 or Coal on Night (2.0 and 2.9).
6. **No pure white and no pure black.** Cream is the lightest tone, Night the darkest.
7. **Gradients have two jobs only:** fading Night over a photo so text is readable, and the soft Ember glow at the foot of the hero. No colour gradients across the page.
8. **Alternate the ground.** Break any run of three sections of the same tone with the other ground or a full-width photo band.
9. **No purple, no thick outlines, no hard shadows.** They belong to the retired direction.

Ember stays an accent, and it already carries the association with saffron, so use it for fire and action, not for flags or ceremony.

| Role | Face | Weights used | Use |
|---|---|---|---|

| Devanagari | **Noto Serif Devanagari** | 500 (400 to 600 loaded) | All Devanagari |

Fallback stacks: Fraunces then Noto Serif Devanagari then `Georgia, serif`. Noto Serif Devanagari then Fraunces then `Georgia, serif`. Hind then `system-ui, -apple-system, "Segoe UI", sans-serif`. Kalam then Hind then `cursive`.

### 5.2 Devanagari rules

From the [Alphabettes guide to Devanagari with Latin](https://www.alphabettes.org/devanagari-typography-101-a-guide-for-typesetting-with-latin/), applied here:

- **Always pair Devanagari with English.** The Devanagari line is character, the English line is the meaning. Never Devanagari alone.
- **Size.** Set Devanagari at the same size as the sentence-case English beside it, or a little larger. Next to all-caps Latin (the labels), make it clearly larger, as the band does, or it looks heavy.
- **Leading.** At least 1.6 for Devanagari. The page sets it by hand on every Devanagari element.
- **Weight.** Use 500. Do not fake bold or italic. Do not add letter-spacing, which breaks the headline stroke.
- **Alignment and wrapping.** Left-aligned, never justified, never hyphenated.
- **Markup.** Every Devanagari element carries `lang="hi"` so browsers and screen readers shape and read it correctly.
- **Review.** A native Hindi speaker must check every phrase before launch.

## 6. Layout and components

### 6.1 Principles

1. **Photography leads.** Type and layout stay out of its way.
2. **Calm and generous.** Wide gutters, deep section padding, short lines.
3. **Soft edges.** Cards 14px, photo frames 10px, buttons fully round.
4. **Quiet ornament.** The inset frame, the diamond bullet and hairlines, nothing more.
5. **Every claim is confirmed or marked TBC.**

## 7. Photography

### 7.1 Direction

Photography does most of the work, so it needs to be real and it needs to be consistent.

- **Light:** the last light after sunset, firelight, string lights, candles and lamp-lit windows. Warm lights against blue-hour sky and dark trees. Avoid flat midday sun and bright white interiors.
- **Subjects:** people gathered, seen from behind, from the side or in silhouette; hands at work (clay, hemp, paint, food); timber, stone, steel, clay and canvas; the valley, the river, wood smoke and tall trees.
- **Mood:** warm, communal and calm. A fire or a lamp as the focal point, dark edges, a warm glow at the centre.
- **Avoid:** posed smiles to camera, stock yoga poses on a sunset peak, heavy filters, anything that looks like a hotel brochure, and any AI-generated image presented as the property.
- **Reference:** [references/images/campfire-forest-gathering.jpg](./images/campfire-forest-gathering.jpg), a mood reference only. It looks AI-generated and is not used on the site.

### 7.2 Treatment

- **Frame it.** Every photo outside a full-width band sits in the inset-rule frame (6.4).
- **Keep colour true.** Grade with a slight warmth in the highlights and cooler shadows. Do not tint, and do not push saturation.
- **Text on a photograph always sits on a Night gradient.** Use the hero's shade as the model. Check contrast against the real image. Cream on a dark gradient should reach 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text, and the lede sits on the darkest part.
- **Crop to the subject, not the formula.** Each `object-position` is set by eye: for example the hero crops to 55% 55% so the fire and the faces stay in frame.
- **One photo is allowed to be quiet.** If a photo is dim, give it space. Do not brighten it to fill the screen.

### 7.3 Technical spec

| Use | Minimum size | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | 2400 by 1350 | 16:9 | Keep a calm, dark area to the lower left for the headline. Subject in the right half |
| Full-width band | 2000 by 1100 | About 2:1 after cropping | Subject in the upper half, so the headline sits below it |
| Programme lead card | 1800 by 1200 | 3:2 | |
| Cards and gallery | 1200 by 800 | 3:2 or 4:3 | |
| Portrait (our idea) | 1200 by 1500 | 4:5 | |
| Mosaic tall | 1400 by 2100 | 2:3 | |

Export as progressive JPEG at about quality 80, in sRGB, with EXIF removed, and aim for under 350KB each. Name files by what they show (`tirthan-smoke.jpg`). Always set `width` and `height`, lazy-load everything below the hero, and mark the hero `fetchpriority="high"`.

### 7.4 Shot list for the real shoot

In priority order:

1. **Hero:** six to ten people around a fire in the last light, from behind or the side, with string lights and the ridgeline.
2. **The place:** the property at dusk with the lights on, the valley, the river, the forest, wood smoke.
3. **Rooms:** warm timber interiors, a bed, the view from the window, the workstation corner.
4. **Sound healing:** bowls and gong by candlelight, hands and mallets.
5. **Yoga:** a small group at first light with the valley behind.
6. **Naturopathy:** the practitioner at work (with consent), herbs, the treatment room.
7. **Workshops:** hands in clay, hemp fibre, paint, plates being designed.
8. **Café:** food on steel and clay, chai, produce from the garden, the kitchen at work.
9. **Villages:** buying milk, fruit and vegetables from neighbours (with consent).
10. **Night:** stars over the forest.
11. **The way in:** the road, and the Aut tunnel if the location is confirmed. International guests look for this.
12. **People:** the founder and practitioners, with consent.

Optional: a silent loop of the fire for the hero, under 3MB, with the still photo as its poster. Reduced-motion visitors see the still.

### 7.5 People and permission

- Get a signed release from anyone recognisable, and a guardian's consent for anyone under 18.
- Ask villagers before photographing them, and say how the photo will be used.
- Stock licences (Pexels, Rawpixel) do not replace a model release.

### 7.6 Alt text

Say what is in the picture, in one sentence, without keywords. Example: "People sitting in cane chairs around a campfire at dusk on a Himalayan hillside, with string lights and mountain ridges under an orange sky." A photo that adds nothing beyond its neighbour text (the dim background of the contact section) takes `alt=""`.

### 7.7 Placeholder photography today

The site currently uses 17 free stock photos, credited in [site/assets/img/photos/CREDITS.md](../site/assets/img/photos/CREDITS.md) and in the page footer.
- Photos under CC BY 2.0 must keep their attribution and the note that they were cropped and colour-graded.
- Do not describe a placeholder as the property or imply a place it is not. Captions say "Placeholder photo".
- When real photography arrives, replace the files, delete the credits, and remove the preview ribbon and the "Placeholder photo" labels.

## 8. Motion and texture

| Effect | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Film grain** | A fixed noise layer over the page at 7% opacity | Gives the dark some tooth |
| **Hero heat** | A soft Ember glow along the bottom of the hero, pulsing between 55% and 100% over 6 seconds | The fire's warmth reaching the page |
| **Embers** | Nine small Glow points drift up the hero over 10 to 14 seconds each, fading out | Atmosphere, kept sparse |
| **Reveal** | Sections fade up 16px over 0.8 seconds as they enter the view | A gentle arrival |
| **Header** | Turns solid after 24px of scroll over 0.3 seconds | Keeps the links readable |
| **Buttons** | 0.2 seconds on hover and press | Feedback |
| **Smooth scroll** | For in-page links | Orientation |

**Not allowed:** parallax, auto-playing carousels, motion that loops fast, or anything that moves behind body text.

**Reduced motion:** with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the grain stays (it is static), the heat, embers and reveals switch off, smooth scroll is off and content shows at once. Without JavaScript, content also shows at once.

## 9. Voice (proposal, to be confirmed)

Plain, warm and specific. A knowledgeable host writing to a guest, not a brochure. Written in English first, because many readers are not native speakers.

**Do**
- Use short sentences and name real things: milk, silk, clay, pine, the river.
- Answer the practical questions early: how far, how long, what a day looks like, what it costs.
- Gloss a Sanskrit or Hindi word the first time: *Prakriti* (nature).
- Say what a guest will do ("Shape pots and objects from clay").
- Write buttons as actions: "Plan your stay", "See the programmes", "Ask about the café".
- Use Indian and British English: programmes, practise (the verb), café, colour. Use km and rupees (₹). Write dates as day month.
- Mark anything unconfirmed with a TBC chip instead of guessing.

**Don't**
- Make medical claims. Say the programmes "support wellbeing". Do not say they treat, cure or heal a condition. Keep the line: "These programmes support wellbeing and do not replace medical care."
- Use stock wellness phrases: transform, detox, find your best self, ancient wisdom, authentic, luxury.
- Use Hindi or Sanskrit as decoration without meaning.
- Promise facilities or figures that are not confirmed: prices, Wi-Fi speed, dates, room counts.

**Lines that set the tone (all from the current page)**
- "Back to the roots."
- "A long weekend, a week, or a month."
- "Practices to slow down with"
- "Make something with your hands"
- "Healing, together."
- "Tall trees and good company."

## 10. Accessibility

- **Contrast:** every pair on the page comes from the table in 4.4. Text over photographs sits on a Night gradient and is checked against the real image.
- **Focus:** always visible. A 2px outline with a 3px offset: Glow on dark grounds and on the volunteering band, Coal on Cream, Paper and the form (Glow is only 1.7:1 on cream).
- **Targets:** primary buttons 3rem high, small buttons and the menu button 2.5rem. Avoid anything smaller for something you tap.
- **Keyboard:** a skip link, a menu button that sets `aria-expanded` and closes on Escape, a logical tab order.
- **Forms:** every field has a visible label. Errors set `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby`, appear as text (not colour alone), and focus moves to the first field with an error. The status message is a polite live region.
- **Structure:** one h1, headings in order, landmarks (header, main, footer, nav) and `lang="hi"` on every Devanagari element.
- **Images:** meaningful alt text. Decorative images use `alt=""`. Embers, grain and glows are `aria-hidden` or pure CSS.
- **Motion:** respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (section 8).
- **No JavaScript:** content and navigation still work.

**Still to check:** the hero text against the real photograph, a screen-reader pass, a test on Safari, Firefox and real phones, and Hindi read aloud by a screen reader.
